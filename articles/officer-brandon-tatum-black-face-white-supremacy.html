Officer Brandon Tatum and the Problem of Putting Black Face on White Supremacy

Semitic Jew • Article
Give

Opening: This Is Not Only About One Case

Officer Brandon Tatum is not dangerous because he is Black and conservative. Black people have the right to think, vote, disagree, and criticize any political party. The problem is not that he disagrees with liberal Black politics. The problem is that his public commentary often functions as a shield for white supremacy while wearing a Black face.

That distinction matters.

“Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment.”

John 7:24

White supremacy has always needed interpreters. It needs people who can make its conclusions sound reasonable, moral, patriotic, and even biblical. In earlier generations, those arguments came directly from plantation preachers, segregationists, colonial theologians, police departments, and politicians. Today, the machinery is more sophisticated. The same system can elevate a Black voice, place him in front of a camera, and use his identity to say what white supremacy wants said without appearing openly racist. This is why Isaiah 5:20 matters.

That is the problem of putting Black face on white supremacy.

It gives the audience permission to say, “See, a Black man said it, so it cannot be anti-Black.” But that is not logic. That is identity laundering. A claim does not become true because a Black person says it. A false argument does not become righteous because it came from someone with brown skin. Scripture does not judge truth by appearance. It judges by fruit, righteousness, and whether a matter is established in truth.

Much of Tatum’s style depends on reframing. When Black people raise questions about policing, the frame becomes victim mentality. When Black people challenge racial hypocrisy, the frame becomes race-baiting. When Black people question the official story, the frame becomes emotionalism. When Black people expose double standards, the frame becomes an attack on law and order. The original question disappears, and the audience is handed a more convenient target. Proverbs 18:17 warns that the first speaker can appear righteous until the matter is searched.

That is not truth. That is narrative control. Proverbs 19:9 warns against false witness and lies.

From an Israelite perspective, this issue is even deeper. Our people have always had to contend not only with foreign oppression, but with internal voices trained to defend captivity. Every empire finds people among the oppressed who are willing to interpret oppression on the empire’s behalf. They become useful because they know the language of the people, understand the wounds of the people, and can redirect the anger of the people away from the system that harmed them. Isaiah 56:10 speaks directly to blind watchmen who cannot warn the people.

That is why this cannot be reduced to “Black conservative versus Black liberal.” That frame is too small. The real question is whether a man’s speech defends truth or protects power. Proverbs 29:26 reminds us that seeking the ruler’s favor is not the same as righteous judgment. Does his speech expose unrighteous judgment, or does it excuse it? Does it give the oppressed tools to reason clearly, or does it train them to distrust their own experience and bow before the same systems that have historically destroyed them?

The Bible does not command Israelites to accept a man because he looks like us. It commands us to judge righteous judgment. Appearance can deceive. Skin color can deceive. Political branding can deceive. A Black face can still carry the logic of white supremacy if the argument serves the same old master.

This is why the phrase “Black face on white supremacy” is not an insult. It is a diagnosis. It describes a public function. It describes what happens when Black identity is used to make white power look innocent. It describes what happens when the suffering of Black people is minimized, mocked, reframed, and dismissed by someone whose presence gives the system racial cover.

Tatum may believe he is simply telling hard truths. But hard truth and useful propaganda are not the same thing. A hard truth corrects a people without selling them back into mental captivity. Propaganda disciplines the oppressed while flattering the powerful. A hard truth demands righteousness from all sides. Propaganda makes the powerless answer for everything while the powerful walk away clean. Leviticus 19:15 forbids honoring the mighty in judgment.

Applause does not make an argument true. Popularity does not make a conclusion righteous. Exodus 23:2 forbids following a multitude to pervert judgment.

Israelites must learn the difference. Hosea 4:6 explains why a people destroyed for lack of knowledge must recover discernment.

The issue is not whether Brandon Tatum has a right to speak. He does. The issue is whether Israelites have enough discernment to test what is being said. We are not obligated to mistake confidence for truth, police background for moral authority, or Black skin for covenant loyalty. Proverbs 23:23 commands us to buy the truth and sell it not.

White supremacy does not become less wicked because it found a Black spokesman.

A lie does not become truth because it learned how to wear our face.
